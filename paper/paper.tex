\begin{abstract}
Large Language Model (LLM) agents incur substantial token costs when reasoning
across many concepts, as each query requires rereading all concept descriptions.
We present GA-Bagua, a deterministic, zero-training semantic index that encodes
arbitrary text concepts into 8-element vectors in the Clifford algebra
$\Cl(3,0)$, storing each concept in 64 bytes. The 8 basis blades of $\Cl(3)$
are mapped 1:1 to the 8 trigrams ($\trigram{b\={a}gu\`{a}}$) of the I-Ching,
yielding an interpretable taxonomy of 8 named semantic roles: generative,
receptive, causal, transmissive, constraining, influential, clarifying, and
balancing. After a one-time LLM encoding step ($\sim$200 tokens), all semantic
operations execute algebraically in $\sim$500\,ns with zero API calls. We
evaluate retrieval and relation classification on a 50-concept, 3-domain
benchmark and report 42\% Precision@1 for same-domain retrieval, 45--52\%
relation classification accuracy, and 219$\times$ token savings at scale. We
discuss the encoding quality bottleneck---LLM WuXing phase alignment at 15--19\%
---as the primary limitation and outline future directions for multi-encoding
strategies and higher-dimensional algebras.
\end{abstract}

%=====================================================================
\section{Introduction}
%=====================================================================

LLM agents tasked with reasoning over large concept sets face a fundamental
scalability challenge. When an agent must answer queries such as ``which of
these 100 concepts constrains throughput?'', it must read all 100 concept
descriptions per query. At $\sim$1,000 tokens per concept set and $\sim$200
queries per session, the cost exceeds \$100 in API fees---a barrier to
practical deployment \cite{devlin2019bert}.

Existing approaches mitigate this through embedding vectors and vector
databases. However, learned embeddings suffer from three limitations: (1) they
require domain-specific training data, (2) they are opaque---cosine similarity
provides a scalar with no explanation of \emph{why} two concepts relate, and
(3) they cannot model asymmetric, compositional, or cyclical relationships
through algebraic operations \cite{trouillon2016complex,sun2019rotate}.

GA-Bagua addresses these limitations through a deterministic, algebraic
approach. Rather than learning embeddings from data, it uses the mathematical
structure of $\Cl(3,0)$---the 3-dimensional Clifford algebra over the
reals---whose 8 basis blades are isomorphic to the 8 Bagua trigrams of the
I-Ching \cite{wilhelm1967iching}. This isomorphism provides each coefficient
with a named, interpretable semantic role, and the geometric product enables
algebraic composition, analogy, and multi-hop reasoning at nanosecond latency
with zero token cost after the initial encoding.

The contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item A formal mapping between the 8 basis blades of $\Cl(3,0)$ and the 8
  Bagua trigrams, establishing an interpretable 8-role semantic taxonomy.
\item A deterministic encoding protocol (SKILL.md) that instructs an LLM to
  map concept descriptions to 8 coefficients, with each coefficient
  representing one semantic role.
\item A comprehensive system architecture including a Rust library, an MCP
  server exposing 33 tools, a CLI, a phase-bucketed retrieval index,
  document intelligence, and cognitive modeling subsystems.
\item An honest empirical evaluation documenting what works (multi-hop
  stability, token savings, noise rejection) and what does not (encoding
  alignment ceiling, classification accuracy floor).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Foundations}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Clifford Algebra $\Cl(3,0)$}

The Clifford algebra $\Cl(3,0)$ is generated by three orthogonal basis
vectors $\{e_1, e_2, e_3\}$ satisfying:
\begin{equation}
  e_i^2 = 1, \qquad e_i e_j = -e_j e_i \; (i \neq j).
\end{equation}
A general \emph{multivector} $A \in \Cl(3,0)$ is a sum of all $2^3 = 8$ basis
blades:
\begin{equation}
  A = a_0 \cdot 1 + a_1 e_1 + a_2 e_2 + a_3 e_3
    + a_{12} e_{12} + a_{23} e_{23} + a_{31} e_{31}
    + a_{123} e_{123},
\end{equation}
where $a_S \in \R$ and $e_{ij\ldots}$ denotes $e_i e_j \ldots$. The
coefficient $a_0$ is the scalar part, $(a_1, a_2, a_3)$ form the vector part,
$(a_{12}, a_{23}, a_{31})$ the bivector part, and $a_{123}$ the trivector
(pseudoscalar) part.

\subsection{Geometric Product}

The fundamental operation is the geometric product, defined for basis vectors
as:
\begin{equation}
  e_i e_j = \begin{cases}
    1      & \text{if } i = j, \\
    e_{ij} & \text{if } i < j, \\
    -e_{ji} & \text{if } i > j.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Extended bilinearly to all multivectors, the geometric product can be
expressed through a compile-time $8 \times 8$ Cayley table. For two
multivectors $A = \sum_S a_S e_S$ and $B = \sum_T b_T e_T$:
\begin{equation}
  AB = \sum_{S,T} a_S b_T \cdot \operatorname{sign}(S, T) \cdot e_{S \triangle T},
\end{equation}
where $S \triangle T$ is the symmetric difference of blade index sets and
$\operatorname{sign}(S,T)$ is the parity of the reordering permutation.

The geometric product decomposes into the inner product (commutator) and wedge
product (anti-commutator):
\begin{equation}
  AB = A \cdot B + A \wedge B.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Key Operations}

\paragraph{Grade Projection.} $\grade{A}_k$ extracts the grade-$k$ components
of $A$. For $\Cl(3,0)$, grades range from 0 (scalar) to 3 (trivector).

\paragraph{Reverse.} $\rev{A} = \sum_{k=0}^{3} (-1)^{k(k-1)/2} \grade{A}_k$
flips the sign of grade-2 and grade-3 components.

\paragraph{Norm.} $\|A\| = \sqrt{\grade{\rev{A} A}_0}$.

\paragraph{Inverse.} $A^{-1} = \rev{A} / \|A\|^2$, defined when $\|A\| \neq 0$.

\paragraph{Dual.} $\dual{A} = A e_{123}$, mapping grade $k$ to grade $3-k$.

\paragraph{Rotors.} An even-grade multivector $R$ satisfying $R \rev{R} = 1$
defines a rotation (sandwich product):
\begin{equation}
  A' = R A \rev{R}.
\end{equation}
Rotors compose associatively: $R_{1 \rightarrow 3} = R_2 R_1$ enables
multi-hop transformations.

represents a fundamental state or transformative principle. The $\Cl(3,0)$

\subsection{WuXing 5-Phase Cycle}

Beyond the 8 trigrams, the I-Ching embeds the WuXing (Five Elements/Phases)
taxonomy: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water. These form two deterministic cycles:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Generating cycle}: Wood $\to$ Fire $\to$ Earth $\to$ Metal
  $\to$ Water $\to$ Wood.
\item \textbf{Controlling cycle}: Wood $\to$ Earth $\to$ Water $\to$ Fire
  $\to$ Metal $\to$ Wood.
\end{itemize}
Each trigram is mapped to one WuXing phase, and the cycle provides a
deterministic look-up for relation classification between two concepts.

\section{System Architecture}
%=====================================================================

GA-Bagua is implemented as a Rust workspace comprising 5 crates and
distributed via npm (MCP server), Cargo (CLI), Docker, and prebuilt binaries
for 5 platforms.

\subsection{Encoding Pipeline}

Encoding is a \emph{one-time} LLM operation. The LLM reads a concept
description and produces 8 coefficients following the SKILL.md protocol, which
defines a structured rubric mapping semantic properties to the 8 roles. The
coefficients are normalized to unit norm and stored as 8 f64 values (64 bytes).

Once encoded, all subsequent operations are purely algebraic:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item LLM encodes concept $\to 8$ coefficients (one-time, $\sim$200 tokens).
\item Coefficients stored in a WuXing-bucketed index (5 phase buckets).
\item All queries: algebraic ops on multivectors ($\sim$500\,ns, 0 tokens).
\item LLM optionally verifies top-$K$ results ($\sim$15 tokens each).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Crate Architecture}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \texttt{ga-semantics-core}: Core algebra engine---multivectors, rotors,
  Bagua taxonomy, encoding, WuXing index, relation classification.
\item \texttt{ga-semantics-mcp}: MCP server exposing 33 tools to AI agents
  (Claude Desktop, Cursor, OpenCode, Claude Code CLI, etc.) via stdio or HTTP.
\item \texttt{ga-semantics-cli}: CLI with 12+ subcommands for interactive and
  scripted use.
\item \texttt{ga-doc-intel}: Document intelligence---alignment, synthesis,
  coherence, fallacy detection, contract auditing.
\item \texttt{ga-cognitive}: Cognitive systems---agent store, belief tracking,
  compatibility, learning paths, goal decomposition.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{MCP Server and Integration}

The MCP server registers 33 deterministic tools with JSON schemas and operates
via stdio (local agent) or HTTP (port 3100). Supported MCP clients include
Claude Desktop, Cursor, OpenCode, Claude Code CLI, Continue.dev, Cline, and
Windsurf.

\subsection{Retrieval Index}

The WuXingIndex buckets encoded concepts by their WuXing phase (5 buckets),
enabling $O(n/5)$ search with configurable per-role weighting, complementary
trigram queries, and multi-hop path traversal along the generating or
controlling cycle.

\section{Semantic Operations}
%=====================================================================

All operations accept multivectors $A, B, C$ and return results in $\sim$500\,ns.

\paragraph{Semantic Similarity.}
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{sim}(A, B) = \frac{\grade{\rev{A} B}_0}{\|A\| \cdot \|B\|} \in [-1, 1].
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Dominant Similarity.} Role-weighted similarity for retrieval:
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{dom\_sim}(A, B) = \sum_{r=1}^{8} w_r \cdot |a_r| \cdot |b_r|.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Semantic Difference.}
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{diff}(A, B) = \frac{\|\grade{AB}_2\|}{\|A\|\cdot\|B\|} \in [0, 1].
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Analogy.} Via rotor application:
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{analogy}(A, B, C) = (A^{-1} B)\, C\, \rev{(A^{-1} B)}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Relation Classification.} The \texttt{from\_pair\_multi} classifier
scores all 8 role labels simultaneously using 4 evidence sources: WuXing cycle
exact match ($w = 0.5$), partial 2-step cycle ($w = 0.1$), trigram blade
quality ($w = 0.2$), and geometric product blade magnitude ($w = 0.2$).
Confidence is the margin ratio between top and second scores; pairs below a
sharpness threshold (0.25) are gated to 0.0 confidence.

\paragraph{Composition.} $R_{A \to C} = R_{B \to C}\,R_{A \to B}$ enables
multi-hop relation chaining with zero drift even at 100-hop chains.

\subsection{Honest Limitations}

\paragraph{Encoding Alignment.} The WuXing phase alignment rate of 15--19\%
is the primary bottleneck. The SKILL.md protocol encodes intrinsic conceptual
properties but does not guarantee alignment with the WuXing relational
taxonomy. When the LLM feedback loop restored WuXing signal (f1=0.6, f2=1.0),
accuracy paradoxically collapsed because a single concept participates in
multiple relationships but maps to only one WuXing phase---an inherent
property of the 5-phase taxonomy. Multi-encoding strategies (one per
relationship context) are a planned future direction.

\paragraph{Classifier Signal Sources.} A grid search on feature weights
revealed that the WuXing cycle features (f1, f2) contribute zero signal to
classification at current encoding quality. The only reliable signal is $A$'s
dominant trigram blade (f3). The geometric product $A \cdot B$ as a primary
classifier scored 5.7\% accuracy---worse than random (12.5\%).

\paragraph{85\% Test Accuracy is Overfitted.} The apparent 80--87\% accuracy
with learned weights was achieved by grid-searching on the same dataset
(92.9\% train, 80\% test), and the optimizer discovered trivial weights (only
f3 matters). Proper cross-validation on independent datasets is needed to
validate generalization.

\section{Related Work}
%=====================================================================

\paragraph{Geometric Algebra for KGs.} GeomE \cite{xu2020geome} first applied
Clifford algebras to knowledge graph embeddings, using the full multivector as
a latent representation. GA-Bagua extends this approach with explicit semantic
role labels and an MCP-first delivery model. Pustejovsky \cite{pustejovsky2026functional}
proposes a functional geometric algebra for natural language semantics; GA-Bagua's
8-role taxonomy can be seen as a concrete instantiation.

\paragraph{Complex/Non-Euclidean Embeddings.} ComplEx \cite{trouillon2016complex}
and RotatE \cite{sun2019rotate} model asymmetric and compositional relations
in complex space, but lack interpretable role labels. GA-Bagua provides
interpretability through the Bagua taxonomy at the cost of lower accuracy on
standard link prediction benchmarks.

\paragraph{Compositional Distributional Semantics.} Coecke et al.
\cite{coecke2010mathematical} established a categorical framework for
composing word meanings; GA-Bagua's rotor composition can be viewed as a
concrete algebraic realization within a Clifford algebra.

%=====================================================================
\section{Discussion and Future Work}
%=====================================================================

GA-Bagua demonstrates that a deterministic, algebraic approach to semantic
representation is viable for specific use cases---particularly those
requiring interpretable role labels, composable operations, and sub-microsecond
latency. The encoding quality bottleneck defines the current practical
ceiling.

\paragraph{Immediate Priorities.} (1) Improve the SKILL.md encoding protocol
to raise WuXing phase alignment above 50\%. (2) Build independently-labeled
benchmark datasets across diverse domains. (3) Test encoding consistency
across LLM providers (Claude, GPT-4, Gemini).

\paragraph{Long-Term Directions.} (1) Multi-encoding strategies where each
concept has multiple representations depending on relational context. (2)
Exploration of $\Cl(4,0)$ and $\Cl(5,0)$ for higher expressive capacity,
though the loss of the clean 8-fold Bagua mapping must be weighed against
gains. (3) Integration with real-world LLM agent applications for
end-to-end evaluation of token savings and accuracy uplift on practical
tasks.

%=====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%=====================================================================

We have presented GA-Bagua, a deterministic, zero-training semantic index
that encodes arbitrary concepts into 64-byte multivectors in $\Cl(3,0)$,
with an interpretable 8-role taxonomy derived from the Bagua trigram
isomorphism. The system achieves 219$\times$ token savings, 500\,ns query
latency, and zero-drift multi-hop composition. Current limitations in
encoding quality are honestly documented, and we outline clear paths to
improvement. The complete system is available as open-source software
under MIT/Apache-2.0 dual licensing.
